# Creator craft playbook (shared training material)

This is the starting curriculum. It is **not** frozen: every agent that learns something with evidence
adds it to its team's ledger, and once a lesson holds across 3 pieces on a channel the strategist
moves it into the team file. Lessons proven on two or more channels move here.

## Where agents learn from (in this order)

1. **Our own data**: the channel's analytics and ledger. Our viewers beat generic advice.
2. **Official platform sources** (check for anything newer than this file each week):
   - YouTube: Official Blog (blog.youtube, Creator & Artist stories, Shorts deep dives), YouTube Help Center (Shorts, analytics, monetization, altered/synthetic content disclosure), YouTube Creators and Creator Insider channels, YouTube Culture & Trends.
   - Instagram: Instagram Creators blog (creators.instagram.com), @creators account, Adam Mosseri's posts on ranking. Note: from 30 Apr 2026 Instagram extended its original-content protections from Reels to photos and carousels; accounts that mostly re-post lose recommendations. Trial Reels show a Reel to non-followers first.
   - Facebook: Meta for Creators / Facebook Creators pages, Meta Professional Dashboard guidance, Meta's content monetization pages.
3. **Breakout pieces in the niche**: outliers from the last 30 days (views far above that account's own average). Study the first 3 seconds, structure, pacing, ending.
4. **Research and established craft**: storytelling (curiosity gap, open loops, setup/payoff), retention editing, practitioner guides. Treat third-party "algorithm secrets" as hypotheses to test, not rules.

## Principles every team starts with

**Hook (0–3 s).** Viewers decide to stay or swipe in the first 1–3 seconds. Show the payoff or the tension, not the setup.
The hook is spoken **and** on screen at frame 0. Question hooks work when the viewer can't guess the answer and cares about it.
Hook families to rotate and test: the surprising number; the "you've been doing X wrong"; the forbidden or hidden ("what they don't tell you");
the challenge ("can you guess…?"); the stake ("this costs you ₹/$ every month"); the story drop-in (start mid-action); the visual shock.

**Build.** Every 3–5 seconds give a new reason to stay: a new fact, a twist, a visual change, an open loop that pays off later.
Cut every word that doesn't move the story. One idea per Short.

**Close.** Pay off the hook in full (never bait without payoff). End with a question that invites a comment, a reason to rewatch (loop ending), or a hook into the next episode.
No long end cards on Shorts.

**Originality.** Original content gets recommended; re-posts, visible third-party watermarks and near-duplicates are demoted. Every piece is made by us.

**Packaging.** Title/caption keyword-rich and true to the video; cover frame readable at thumbnail size; 3–5 relevant hashtags at most.

**Cadence.** Steady, predictable posting beats bursts. Posting time comes from each account's own best-time data.

**Community.** Reply to every comment in the first hour where possible; pin a question; turn good comments into the next video.

## Metrics that matter (read in this order)
1. First-3-second hold / skip rate (Instagram skip rate; YouTube "stayed to watch" from Studio and engaged-view share from Windsor).
2. Average % viewed and rewatches.
3. Shares and saves (strongest signal of value), then comments, then likes.
4. Follows/subscribers per 1,000 views.
5. Reach to non-followers (is the platform recommending us?).

## RCA method (Data Miner)
Symptom → which metric broke (hook, middle, ending, packaging, topic, timing) → 5 whys to a root cause → one change → how we'll know (metric and threshold) → log it.
Small samples (under about 100 views or fewer than 3 pieces) are "not enough evidence", never a trend.
